## Slide 2: Plan before going outdoors · 0:25–0:37 (12 seconds)

**SHOW:** Plan time outdoors → Your outdoor plan

**SAY:**

The pathway starts before illness. Choose where and when you will be outdoors, and what you plan to do. Prevention belongs here.


## Slide 3: Prepare, check, and respond · 0:37–0:55 (18 seconds)

**SHOW:** Learn & Prevent → Generate My Outdoor Tick-Prevention Plan

**SAY:**

Prepare suitable clothing and an EPA-registered repellent, following product instructions. After outdoor activities, check your body, clothes, gear, and pets. Shower within two hours. These precautions support risk reduction; they do not guarantee protection.


## Slide 4: After a tick bite · 0:55–1:19 (24 seconds)

**SHOW:** After a tick bite → Remove → Record → Seek advice

**SAY:**

If a tick is attached, use fine-tipped tweezers, grasp close to the skin, and pull upward steadily. Record when and where it happened. Seek medical advice promptly when appropriate, including after some high-risk bites. Do not wait for symptoms if you need prevention advice.


## Slide 5: Record when and where · 1:19–1:29 (10 seconds)

**SHOW:** Prepare for care → Tick encounter details

**SAY:**

Keep the date and possible exposure location in your record. That place may differ from where you live or need care.


## Slide 6: Tell the story once · 1:29–1:51 (22 seconds)

**SHOW:** Prepare for care → Tell us what happened → Organize My Story

**SAY:**

Now, a fictional person feels tired after an outdoor walk and wants help preparing for care. They enter a short story, including payments, missed work, and unpaid help. PathwayAI organizes the details for them to check, change, and download.


## Slide 7: Prepare questions about testing · 1:51–2:05 (14 seconds)

**SHOW:** Care & Support → Lyme tests → Before the test

**SAY:**

The testing guide helps people prepare questions about symptoms, timing, the complete report, and costs. It supports a conversation with a clinician; it does not diagnose.


## Slide 8: Include daily life and caregiver experience · 2:05–2:17 (12 seconds)

**SHOW:** Care & Support → Daily life and caregiver experience

**SAY:**

Some people need help reporting. Caregiver observations, the person’s daily-life difficulties, and the caregiver’s own experience can be recorded separately. Partial answers are welcome.


## Slide 9: Review payments, work and unpaid help · 2:17–2:39 (22 seconds)

**SHOW:** Care & Support → Review and download your visit and burden card

**SAY:**

Here, the fictional person paid one hundred eighty dollars for care and forty-five dollars for travel: two hundred twenty-five dollars altogether. Three missed workdays and eight hours of unpaid help stay separate. Unknown answers stay unknown.


## Slide 10: Prepare a request for support · 2:39–2:54 (15 seconds)

**SHOW:** Care & Support → Choose the help you need → Getting to care

**SAY:**

A record becomes more useful when it supports a next step. Here, someone can prepare a request for transport help, asking about eligibility, cost, booking, and a return trip.


## Slide 11: Prepare for a benefits conversation · 2:54–3:06 (12 seconds)

**SHOW:** Care & Support → Prepare for a benefits conversation

**SAY:**

Benefits preparation helps people organize questions and relevant records for food assistance or disability support. The agency determines eligibility. Nothing is submitted automatically.


## Slide 12: Compare county evidence · 3:06–3:22 (16 seconds)

**SHOW:** Community Burden & Action → Compare Dutchess and Orange

**SAY:**

Now we move to community evidence. We compare Dutchess and Orange counties using New York reports, federal CDC, Census and HRSA context, and published research. Source years and limits remain visible.


## Slide 13: Explore a cost illustration · 3:22–3:37 (15 seconds)

**SHOW:** Community → Estimated community cost: Dutchess and Orange

**SAY:**

The two-county cost illustration is about four point one million dollars in twenty sixteen dollars. It multiplies reported cases by a study mean. It is not measured county spending.


## Slide 14: Connect needs with possible actions · 3:37–3:55 (18 seconds)

**SHOW:** Community → Patient voices → community action

**SAY:**

The next question is practical: what support should a community explore? Patients, caregivers, and willing partners could review transport barriers, agree on a feasible response, and track rides, appointments, and needs still unmet.


## Slide 15: Summarize comparable reports · 3:55–4:10 (15 seconds)

**SHOW:** Community → See how comparable patient reports could be summarized

**SAY:**

These six records are invented. Five have usable answers; one leaves fields unknown. They show how comparable reports could be summarized, not what everyone in the county experiences.


## Slide 16: Ask about the public evidence · 4:10–4:33 (23 seconds)

**SHOW:** Community → Ask AI about the public evidence

**SAY:**

Optional AI explains a curated public evidence summary without receiving private patient cards. Story AI requires permission; the local organizer works without it.

AI can organize information, but people review its meaning. Data can reveal patterns, but it cannot replace lived experience.
