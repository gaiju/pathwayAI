As a pathologist, I studied disease through a microscope. As an epidemiologist, I looked for patterns across communities.

Then it became personal.

I lost my job, and my family lost our health insurance. The next month, my husband was bitten by a tick and developed Lyme disease.

For my husband, speaking to a doctor in English could feel overwhelming. I wanted to make that first conversation easier.

I'm Yuwei Zhang. That experience led me to build PathwayAI.

A medical bill shows only part of what illness costs. There is time spent finding care, work missed, and help from family. PathwayAI is a working web app that connects practical patient support with evidence for community planning.

First, what does a patient gain?

Start with the question my family faced: where do you go without insurance? In Care and Support, people can find community health centers and questions to ask about income-based fees, coverage, and hospital financial assistance. Care is not automatically free. But they have somewhere to start.

Support also starts before illness. In Learn and Prevent, enter a destination ZIP code, month, and outdoor activity. Download a practical tick-prevention plan. On Tick Bite: What Next, find removal instructions, what to record, and when to contact a clinician.

Early, appropriate treatment can help prevent more severe Lyme disease. Published research reports higher costs for disseminated disease. Helping people reach care is one reason we address language, insurance, and cost barriers.

Now imagine someone preparing for a visit. Every patient detail in this demonstration is fictional.

They enter what happened and review a timeline of exposure, symptoms, tests, and care. Symptom onset and diagnosis delay stay separate. They can correct the summary, download it, and bring it with their original test report.

People can write in their own language, or mix languages. With permission, optional external AI helps prepare an English summary alongside the original for review. The aim is to help them feel better prepared to explain symptoms and ask questions, so language is less of a barrier to seeking timely care. It does not replace a qualified interpreter.

The English organizer also works without external AI. This demonstration uses that option.

And the cost record? Adding numbers alone will not pay a bill.

Our fictional patient paid 180 dollars for care and 45 dollars for travel, missed three workdays, and received eight hours of family help. Money, workdays, and caregiving hours stay separate. The value is having these concerns together when asking for support.

Choose Download my visit summary for the visit note. Under Review or add costs and time, choose Download appointment and cost summary. The PDFs save to your device. The care-access checklist provides questions for the clinic or billing office.

Second, what does a community gain?

A county team needs to decide where to focus limited resources. In Community Burden and Action, we compare Dutchess and Orange counties.

New York surveillance supplies reported Lyme rates. Census data adds population and language context. HRSA describes healthcare resources. CDC tick surveillance informs exposure context, and CDC PLACES adds broader community-health estimates. CMS provides separate Original Medicare payment context, not Lyme-specific costs.

Sources and years stay visible. Published Lyme cost estimates illustrate possible burden; they are not measured county spending.

What can a county do next?

The planning brief connects a need with possible actions, partners, and measures. That could mean tick education in parks, helping uninsured residents reach existing clinics, or improving connections to language and transportation support. Partners would confirm service capacity and resources before starting.

Third, how do these two sides connect?

County averages cannot explain why one family struggled to reach care. Patient experiences can reveal questions worth investigating. Public data helps put those questions in context. Together, they can inform a more focused response.

Personal notes are not automatically sent to counties. Shared collection is off in this demo. Secure, consent-based reporting is a future pilot goal.

People living with Lyme disease and a county health commissioner reviewed the working app. Their feedback helped us simplify choices and clarify next steps.

We are seeking patient groups, health centers, and community partners for a small pilot. We would test whether summaries are useful, people reach support, and needs remain unmet, before claiming better outcomes or savings. Our proposed model keeps patient access free through grants and partnerships.

AI can help organize words. Data can reveal patterns. Patients remain at the center.
