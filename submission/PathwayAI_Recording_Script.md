# PathwayAI recording script: what to show and what to say


Use the nine-slide visual deck for the simplest recording. The same sequence works with the live app. Left-column directions are not narration. The plain-text narration file remains free of stage directions.


## Recording software

- Prepared deck: use Microsoft PowerPoint Record, then export an MP4 video if supported by your version. Presenter View shows the complete speech in speaker notes.
- Live app: share the app window in Zoom and use computer recording where your account and desktop client permit it. Confirm that the microphone and screen are captured in a short test.
- Built-in alternatives: Windows Snipping Tool screen recording or macOS Shift-Command-5. Check microphone settings; capabilities vary by version.

Use fictional entries. Close unrelated tabs, notifications and personal information. Rehearse the entire recording with a timer; the 605-word narration plus live clicking may exceed five minutes. Keep the screenshots ready and edit pauses if the submission rules allow.


## Fictional story to paste

I felt tired after an outdoor walk and would like help preparing for a visit. I paid $180 for medical care and $45 for travel. I missed 3 workdays. My unpaid caregiver spent 8 hours.

**APP PAGE:** Start page

**SHOW / CLICK:** Keep the opening page visible while introducing the three ideas and your background.

**SAY:**

A patient’s story.

A community’s evidence.

A practical next step.

Today, these often live in different places.

What if we could bring them together, with the patient at the center?

That is PathwayAI.

I’m Yuwei Zhang from Team GreenAcorn. My background is in pathology, with experience at the National Institutes of Health and the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services.

My experience taught me to examine evidence. Patients taught me to ask what it misses.

A medical bill can tell us what a visit cost. It cannot tell us what it took to get there.

The travel. The missed work. The unpaid help. The effort of telling the same story again.

Let me show you how PathwayAI makes that journey visible.


## Scene 2 / deck slide 2: First: the patient story

**APP PAGE:** Care & Support

**SHOW / CLICK:** Click Prepare for care. Under Tell us what happened, paste the fictional story. Leave external AI off and click Organize My Story. Choose Past 30 days in the cost section.

**SAY:**

First, the patient’s story.

This is a fictional example. A person feels tired after an outdoor walk and wants help preparing for care.

Over thirty days, they paid one hundred eighty dollars for care and forty-five dollars for travel, missed three workdays, and received eight hours of unpaid help.

They write a short story. PathwayAI helps organize the details into a record they can check, change, and download.


## Scene 3 / deck slide 3: The reviewed personal card

**APP PAGE:** Care & Support

**SHOW / CLICK:** Open Review and download your visit and burden card. Show $180 care, $45 other payments, 3 missed workdays, 8 unpaid-help hours, and the $225 payment total.

**SAY:**

Two hundred twenty-five dollars paid. Three workdays missed. Eight hours of unpaid help.

We keep those measures separate. If something is unknown, it stays unknown.


## Scene 4 / deck slide 4: A useful request for support

**APP PAGE:** Care & Support

**SHOW / CLICK:** Under Choose the help you need, select Getting to care. Show A request you can use now and Download my support request. Do not send anything.

**SAY:**

They can bring the summary to a visit, prepare a transport request, or use a benefits checklist. Nothing is sent automatically. They choose what to share.


## Scene 5 / deck slide 5: Second: community evidence

**APP PAGE:** Community Burden & Action

**SHOW / CLICK:** Click Explore community burden. Find Compare Dutchess and Orange counties. Keep Reported Lyme rate selected. Point to the reporting year.

**SAY:**

Second, the community’s evidence.

Here we compare Dutchess and Orange counties in New York. We use CDC, Census, and HRSA data alongside New York surveillance and published research. Together, these sources provide context about reported disease, healthcare resources, community conditions, and economic burden.

Each source tells part of the story. None tells all of it.

County averages do not describe every resident. One person does not represent the county. PathwayAI keeps these views separate, but makes both visible.


## Scene 6 / deck slide 6: Third: an action to evaluate

**APP PAGE:** Community Burden & Action

**SHOW / CLICK:** Find Patient voices → community action. Keep Getting to care as the priority. Point to the proposed action, people who could help, and measures.

**SAY:**

Patient advocates reminded us to look beyond bills: unpaid care, daily life, and the effort of reaching support. Their feedback helped shape the caregiver options, simpler story entry, and support-preparation tools.

Third, a practical next step.


## Scene 7 / deck slide 7: Comparable reports, clearly labeled

**APP PAGE:** Community Burden & Action

**SHOW / CLICK:** Open See how comparable patient reports could be summarized. Scroll to Synthetic summary of all six cards. Show the fictional label, five usable answers and one missing answer. Then point back to the proposed response or brief download.

**SAY:**

This demonstration uses six invented records. Five report transportation barriers. One leaves the answer blank. These are examples, not collected patient findings.

What could a community do with real, comparable reports?

A care-navigation team could investigate help arranging suitable rides. Patients and caregivers would help decide whether that is the right priority. Local partners would check what they can provide.

The downloadable planning brief names a proposed action, potential partners, and measures.

Rides requested. Rides completed. Appointments attended. Needs still unmet.

Make burden visible. Plan a response. Learn whether it helps.


## Scene 8 / deck slide 8: AI with human review

**APP PAGE:** Community Burden & Action

**SHOW / CLICK:** Open Ask AI about the public evidence. Show the question selector and explanation. A live response is optional: only show one if it succeeds, and identify generated text as AI output. Otherwise show the question without implying a response occurred.

**SAY:**

Here, a person can ask AI a focused question about the public evidence. It uses a curated summary, keeps source links available, and does not receive private patient cards. Optional story AI requires permission. The local organizer works without it.

AI can organize information, but people review its meaning.

Data can reveal patterns, but it cannot replace lived experience.

Models can explore possibilities, but people remain responsible for decisions.

The patient remains at the center.
