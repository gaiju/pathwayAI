Start before a bite. Continue through care, burden, and community action.

Choose where and when you will be outdoors, and what you plan to do. Prevention starts with preparation.

Prepare protective clothing and an EPA-registered repellent. Follow product instructions. Afterwards, check your body, clothing, gear, and pets. Shower within two hours. These steps reduce risk; they cannot guarantee protection.

If a tick is attached, grasp close to the skin with fine-tipped tweezers and pull upward steadily. Record when and where. Some high-risk bites need prompt clinical advice. Do not wait for symptoms if you need prevention advice.

Keep the date and exposure location. These may differ from where you live or need care.
