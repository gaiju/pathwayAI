As a pathologist, I studied disease through a microscope. As an epidemiologist, I studied patterns across communities.

Then it became personal.

After I lost my job, my family lost our health insurance. The next month, my husband was bitten by a tick and became ill with Lyme disease.

Overnight, our lives were filled with questions. Where could we find affordable care? What would the tests cost? And how could he explain his symptoms in a language he wasn’t comfortable speaking?

I also helped another family navigate disability benefits. When someone is ill and English is limited, understanding forms and explaining daily limitations can feel overwhelming.

Those experiences led me to build PathwayAI. It brings prevention, language assistance, and practical support into one place—and helps show what illness costs a family.

But being told to see a doctor is only the beginning. What if you have no insurance? What if you’re worried about the bill?

Helping people overcome those barriers matters to families—and their communities.

[NAVIGATE: Care & Support → 1. Find care → open care resources.]

In Care & Support, people can find community health centers and questions to ask about financial assistance. Patients should check available services, interpreter support, and costs with the provider before their appointment.

[NAVIGATE: 2. Prepare for your visit → preferred language / AI assistance. Use your tested example.]

Next, they can put their story into words.

They can write in their preferred language, or mix languages while keeping English place names and medical terms.

If they choose, the app’s built-in AI helps organize their notes into an English summary alongside the original. They review and correct it before sharing.

The summary brings together possible exposure, symptoms, and any tests or care already received. Then they select “Download my visit summary.”

[SHOW: Previously downloaded visit-summary PDF. Then return to the app.]

Here’s the PDF they can bring to a first appointment or follow-up visit, along with any test reports they already have.

Keeping dated records can also help them prepare for future support or disability-benefits conversations.

[NAVIGATE: Care & Support → 4. Bills and costs → your example record. Keep example figures visible.]

Now, let’s look beyond the medical bill.

Here’s an example: 180 dollars for care, 45 dollars for travel, three missed workdays, and eight hours of unpaid caregiving.

We keep money, workdays, and caregiving hours separate. Each captures a different part of the household burden.

[NAVIGATE: 5. Benefits and everyday support.]

If everyday needs become difficult to manage, there are also resources for food, housing, transportation, and bills.

AI can organize a story, but it cannot replace human judgment. Public data reveals patterns—but patient voices give those patterns meaning.

PathwayAI brings them together, keeping patients at the center.

So, what can a community do with this?

The community view combines CDC, Census, HRSA, and CMS open data with New York Lyme surveillance. It shows reported illness alongside healthcare resources and local access barriers.
