# PathwayAI: support update and NIH alignment

Reviewed October 17, 2026 against the NIH TOPx HHS Tech Sprint guidance supplied by the project lead that day. A direct request to the NIH website was blocked by this environment's proxy (HTTP 403), so live-page changes could not be independently checked.

Official page: https://www.nih.gov/challenges/topx-hhs-tech-sprint-ai-invisible-illness

The Cost of Illness challenge asks teams to use U.S. Open Data and AI to make human and economic costs visible and measurable. Its Phase 2 rubric has four equally weighted criteria. This review describes alignment, not an award prediction or certification.

| Criterion | Working demonstration | Limits and next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Results for the American People | Organize a story; review costs, time and daily-life effects; prepare a request for care, food, housing or disability-benefits help. | Evaluate whether people reach useful support. Downloads do not establish benefit eligibility or completed referrals. |
| Data, Tech & Evidence-Driven Innovation | Federal and state context, published cost references, explicit calculations, local story rules and optional consented external AI. | Patient experiences remain separate from population estimates. Existing studies do not capture the full burden of delayed diagnosis. |
| Execution, Efficiency & Delivery | Downloadable personal card, visual, support summary, benefits checklist and community planning brief. Reuse selected entries rather than ask again. | Session-based records; secure storage across visits and automatic community aggregation remain future work. |
| Scalability, Reuse & Societal Impact | Support preparation and reporting logic can inform adaptation to other illnesses. | TXT and SVG downloads are not FHIR or other health-system interoperability. Integration needs design and validation. |

## Support changes

Care & Support now offers six plain-language needs, including food or housing and disability benefits. It provides relevant starting links and a request the person can use. A collapsed support-summary tool reuses selected existing facts only when chosen; it can include the existing visit summary by separate choice. Users review and edit the text before downloading. Nothing is sent automatically.

The update uses themes from community feedback. It does not publish private correspondence, treatment claims or personal cost figures, or add them to county estimates.

## Demo emphasis

Keep the 4:45 demonstration focused on the organized story, personal cost-and-time card and community planning brief. Briefly show that recorded needs can lead to support preparation. Describe secure longitudinal records and consent-based local reporting as next steps. Keep research assumptions and working-versus-future boundaries explicit; health improvements and savings require evaluation.
