As a pathologist, I studied disease through a microscope. As an epidemiologist, I looked for patterns across communities. Then, three years ago, it became personal. I lost my job, and my family lost our health insurance. The next month, my husband was bitten by a tick and developed Lyme disease.

That experience taught me how difficult it can be to navigate care while worrying about costs—and how language can make those conversations even harder. I kept thinking: how could we help other families feel better prepared?

I’m Yuwei Zhang. Those lessons led me to build PathwayAI. Illness costs more than a medical bill. There is time spent finding care, missed work, and help from family.

PathwayAI connects practical patient support with public evidence for community planning. First, what does a patient gain?

Start in Care and Support. Someone without insurance can find community health centers and questions about income-based fees, coverage, and hospital financial assistance. Care is not automatically free, but they have somewhere to start.

Support also begins before illness. In Learn and Prevent, enter a destination ZIP code, month, and outdoor activity. Download a practical tick-prevention plan.

On Tick Bite: What Next, find removal instructions, what to record, and when to contact a clinician. Some bites require time-sensitive advice. Early, appropriate treatment can help prevent more severe Lyme disease. Addressing barriers to care is part of addressing the burden. Now imagine someone preparing for a visit. They record exposure, symptoms, tests, and care, then review and correct a timeline. Symptom onset and diagnosis delay stay separate. Original test reports still matter.

People can write in their preferred language or mix languages. With permission, optional external AI helps prepare an English summary alongside the original for review.

The goal is to help people feel better prepared to explain symptoms and ask questions, so language is less of a barrier to seeking care. It does not replace a qualified medical interpreter. We have invited Mandarin-, Spanish-, and English-speaking reviewers to test whether instructions are clear and summaries preserve their meaning. That testing is ongoing.

The English organizer also works without external AI. That is the option shown here. Select Download my visit summary to save the reviewed note. What about costs? Adding numbers alone will not pay a bill.

Our fictional patient paid 180 dollars for care and 45 dollars for travel, missed three workdays, and received eight hours of family help.

Money, workdays, and caregiving hours stay separate. The benefit is keeping these concerns together when asking for support.

Download the appointment and cost summary. The care-access checklist provides questions for a clinic or billing office. Second, what does a community gain?

County teams need to decide where to focus limited resources. Community Burden and Action brings together evidence for that conversation.

New York surveillance provides reported Lyme cases and rates. Census data adds population and language context. HRSA describes healthcare resources. CDC tick surveillance informs exposure context, while CDC PLACES adds broader community-health estimates. CMS provides separate Original Medicare spending context—not Lyme-specific costs.

The county illustration multiplies 2024 reported cases by a published societal-cost benchmark in 2016 dollars. Sources and calculations stay visible. This is an illustration, not measured county spending. The planning brief connects a need with possible actions, partners, and measures: prevention outreach, clinic navigation, or connections to language and transportation support.

Partners would confirm capacity and resources. A pilot could track completed referrals, appointments reached, assistance received, and unmet needs. Third, how do these connect?

County averages cannot explain why one family struggled to reach care. Patient experiences reveal questions worth investigating. Public data provides context for a focused response.

Personal notes are not automatically sent to counties. Secure, consent-based reporting is a future goal. People living with Lyme disease and a county health commissioner reviewed the working app. Their feedback helped us simplify choices and clarify next steps.

We welcome patient groups, health centers, and community partners for a small pilot. We will evaluate usefulness before claiming better outcomes or savings. Our proposed model keeps patient access free through grants and partnerships. AI can help organize words. Data can reveal patterns. Patients remain at the center. PathwayAI.
